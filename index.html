Passage (Set 11): In the middle of the night, Shankar was waken up by a strange loud noise from the forest. There seemed to be a huge turmoil in the jungles. Alvarez too had been up. They listen carefully - and what they heard was truly strange. They had no clue about what was happening. Shankar switched on his torch and was about to leave the tent, but Alvarez stopped him. He said, "Don't rush out in these unknown jungles at night. I have cautioned you many times. Besides, where is your riffle?" It was pitched dark outside. What they saw in the torch light defied logic. Wild animals were running desperately, trampling on bushes and small plants. They came out of the mountain forest on the west and ran towards the hill on the east. There were hyenas, baboons and wild buffalos. Two leopards passed close by them.

Passage (Set 12): Seventy seven years old Shiv Charan Yadav has been burning the midnight oil for the past forty six years in repeated attempts to clear the secondary school examination at Khohari village near Behror in Alwar district. Not one to accept failure, he will be taking the exam for the forty-seventh time this year. The Rajasthan Board of Secondary Education has proven a tough nut for him to crack. He has failed forty six times. One Thursday, he exuded confidence about success in his forty-seventh attempt, "I appeared for the first time in 1968. I failed that year. Each time I take the exam, I fail in one or the other subjects. This time I hope to pass in all. I have taken classes from some school teachers. This time I hope to clear the exam." Yadav lost his mother when he was two months old. His father died when he was ten. His uncle and his other family members brought him up.

Passage (Set 13): The tomb of Humayun, the second Mughal emperor was built between 1569 and 1571 by the Persian architect Mirak Mirza Ghiyath under the instructions of Humayun's grieving widow, Hamida Banu Begum. The oldest garden-tomb in the Indian sub-continent, it is the first example of the Persian influence on Indian architecture. It houses more than 1000 graves, which has led to it being called 'the Dormitory of the Mughals'. It houses the tombs of the Mughal emperors, Jahandar Shah and Farrukh Siyar, and also of Darah Shikoh - Shahjahan's murdered son. The tomb is a protected monument which means the defacing it in any way would cost you Rs. 5000 or three months in prison. It is on the world Heritage list, and recognized internationally as a monument that deserves protection for the benefit of all humanity. A massive restoration process was carried out over four years to ensure that the maintenance of the tomb met international standards. Between the years 2000 and 2003, the Agha Khan Trust for Culture, in collaboration with the Archaeological Survey of India, carried out a project to revitalise the 30 acre garden and the monument itself. The tomb is located near the shrine of an important Sufi Saint, Nizamuddin Auliya. It is considered auspicious to be buried near the tomb of a saint, and there are various other monuments outside the main tomb.
